# Final-E limitations draft

The evidence concerns held-out entities within a fixed five-topic benchmark and two instruction-tuned models. It does not establish unseen-topic or unrestricted compositional generalization, nor unfamiliarity to the pretrained language models. Topic support is uneven, especially the large cities contribution; pooled and fixed-five-topic macro results address different weighting choices. Positive macro gain does not establish improvement in every topic or sample.

B25 supplies 25 structured pair blocks per sample, expanded to 400 dependent compound rows, alongside substantial atomic supervision. This is a fixed tested allocation, not evidence of a minimum sufficient label budget or sample efficiency. Compound-guided selection already uses compound labels. The improvement estimates the benefit of the tested learned-readout procedure over the tested finite bank and selector; it does not show that information lies outside the collective span of that bank.

The restored-full-training sensitivity changes atomic exposure, preprocessing and balanced sampling together. It cannot isolate a pure withholding intervention. S_full still uses compound labels for selection; full-training repair was not tested. D-selected bank and clean-compound references remain descriptive and do not authorize an E-based comparator search. Differences in significance labels across models do not establish an interaction.

Retention means AUROC loss stays within the declared margin under the frozen interval rule. Qwen's atomic differences are small and negative, with wholly negative intervals, despite satisfying the .005 retention margin. A claim of zero atomic loss would be false. AUROC retention does not demonstrate stable probabilities, calibrated confidence, unchanged fixed decisions or behavioral truthfulness. No deletion ablation establishes that the atomic-loss term causes retention; such a mechanism claim is omitted. The proposed fixed-layer deletion study would not automatically explain R_all.

Wording and constituent outcomes limit claims of robustness. Both alternate OR wordings reduce the learned heads' pooled OR-boundary point estimates, with an unresolved paired sign for one Llama sample/wording comparison. Constituent transfer includes point-below-.90, uncertain-bound, unresolved-.05 and material-deficit classifications. The complete secondary sources retain these outcomes, all topics and AB/BA orders. Reporting dependent row counts is an inventory of the report, not a count of independent findings. Point-only order-specific records do not gain invented uncertainty intervals here.

The bootstrap resamples entities within separate fixed atomic and compound frames, preserves observed support and conditions on the frozen fits and selectors. It does not include training, model-pretraining or selection uncertainty. All intervals are pointwise. The conjunction of primary gain, practical gain, macro support, full-training sensitivity and retention checks provides neither simultaneous nor joint 95% confidence. Undefined results must remain unresolved under the frozen finite-draw rule, even though the accepted audit reported no undefined point/interval rows in the existing report.

Linear decodability does not establish that the language model causally uses the decoded information when answering. Behavior, matched-chat, calibration, causal interventions and loss-deletion fits remain deferred. No claim of model reasoning failure follows from these readout results. No literature citation or novelty claim is introduced in this draft.

The scientific audit establishes exact replay of all report rows and predictions, with a separate implementation for specified principal calculations. Its two coverage statements must remain distinct; not every reported field has an independent implementation. Mac 1 and Mac 2 used the same physical hardware. This paper preparation is a presentation check against accepted outputs, not another scientific audit or hardware replication.
