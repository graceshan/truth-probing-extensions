What the R squared result does and does not say
An item-level held-out R² near 0.45 leaves substantial individual-score variation unexplained. It does not, by itself, tell us whether the model predicts AUROC accurately. AUROC is the probability that a randomly drawn positive score outranks a negative score, with half credit for ties. A model can predict that probability well without predicting each item accurately.
Cell means and spreads can be sufficient for an approximate AUROC prediction under a suitable distributional model, such as Gaussian score distributions. Without those assumptions, tails, multimodality, and other distributional features can matter too. The practical concern is therefore the ability to predict new cell distributions, including their spreads, rather than the numerical size of the item-level R² alone.
Three constituent AND and OR is the first test
The connectives are unchanged, so 3-way AND/OR does not require inventing a new operator rule. It does require a specified extension from two to three inputs: how the pooling signal, positional weights, length offset, and noise behave. Binary data may not identify those choices uniquely. Freeze them before observing ternary probe scores. XOR and mixed scope need additional rules and remain secondary.
Why Route A still gets the main October allocation
Selection and repair have direct, testable outcomes on the existing protocol and can largely reuse cached states. Route B has a plausible explanatory target, but its crucial extrapolation is still untested. The schedule decision is about the risk of predicting new distributions in the remaining time; it is not a rejection of pooling. Section 10 specifies a bounded experiment that could make Route B much stronger.

Executable repair and atomic only objectives
Fit one linear logistic head per layer, shared across AND and OR. It receives only the saved hidden state; no explicit operator indicator, constituent scores, or parse is appended. The intercept is free and unpenalized. The following values are proposed implementation defaults to freeze before the curves, not settings optimized on final results.
LR₀ = mean loss on P + 0.001 ‖w‖²
Lrepair = 0.5 ℓP + 0.5 ℓA compound + 0.001 ‖w‖2
Latomic = 0.5 ℓP + 0.5 ℓA isolated + 0.001 ‖w‖2
R₀ uses the full mean P loss plus the same penalty: set the A weight to zero and renormalize the remaining data weight to one. Leaving a half-weight P block would silently double effective regularization. Here each barred loss is mean binary cross-entropy, and the penalty applies only to w. The two adaptation objectives give equal weight to the P block and the new A block. Thus λ = 0.5 and ρ = 0.001 stay fixed across budgets, layers, models, and folds. Increasing B changes the number of facts rather than silently changing the relative data weight or penalty.

Freeze practical tolerances
The proposed protocol uses two separately named margins: δ_RS = 0.02 for repair minus selection and δ_CS = 0.02 for C_clean minus selection. Two AUROC points correspond to two additional correctly ranked positive-negative comparisons per hundred, with half credit for ties. This is a proposed practical judgment, not a data-validated cutoff. Confirm its scientific meaning at design freeze; document any change before E, without choosing a margin to make a result pass.
On October 2, estimate paired interval widths on D at the planned E topic/entity counts using available matched scores; mark this as a precision proxy. Refresh it with the B = 25 D table before final lock. A half-width near 0.02 leaves little room for a positive repair gain while requiring an upper endpoint below 0.02. Do not promise a decisive sufficiency result or widen the margin to make it reachable. Freeze whether sufficiency is a well-powered decision or an exploratory classification; in either case report both effect sizes and intervals. A positive lower 95% endpoint establishes superiority; one above δ_RS establishes a gain exceeding the practical tolerance.

Operational rule for selection sufficiency
For each model at B = 25, call selection sufficient on the bare-OR boundary only if E supports every criterion: paired 95% upper endpoints below δ_RS for R_all − S_all and below δ_CS for C_clean − S_all; C_clean’s boundary AUROC lower endpoint at least 0.90; and S_all atomic/AND retention supported within tolerance. These are bare-template criteria. Wording transfer remains a required core result: evaluate the locked methods on the held-out AND and OR templates and report gains, gaps, and retention for each. The 0.90 requirement is an operational standard for the word sufficient; a lower control score can still show useful headroom and support a narrower result. Keep crossover descriptive unless jointly supported
The hypothesis is early selection gains at 5–10 pairs and a repair crossover within 10–50. The descriptive crossover is the first tested budget where mean R_all exceeds S_all and remains higher at larger completed budgets. If selection matches repair
Apply the Section 5 sufficiency rule: small upper bounds on repair-minus-selection and control-minus-selection, a successful C_clean, and retained atomic/AND performance. If a repaired direction wins
Center R_all versus S_all and place both against the bank oracle and C_clean. Repair below the oracle is an adaptation-efficiency result; exceeding observed bank scores is a narrower claim than information outside their span. If scalar decoding or prompting resolves the gap
