9 How the results determine the next decision
Use the inherited .02 sufficiency margins and retention tolerances. The proposed constituent planning targets are .90 for each of the two marginal and four conditional equal-topic AUROCs, and .05 for each matched-layer transfer loss. They guide development decisions and are not scientific constants or acceptance criteria. Freeze them before new scores.
Constituent success requires six endpoints and three comparisons
Assess the six-endpoint vector separately for source-trained heads on source D, those same heads on target D, and target-trained heads on target D at the same layer. The latter two use identical states. “Useful transfer” requires strong absolute source, target and transferred decoding; closeness to a weak target reference cannot qualify. Report each topic as well as the equal-topic endpoints.
All six point estimates at least .90 in all three comparisons is a promising absolute-performance pattern. Lower pointwise bounds at least .90 strengthen that judgment; if a bound crosses .90, label absolute performance uncertain at that endpoint. Require upper paired bounds below .05 on all six target-minus-source losses for the planned noninferiority classification. This is one-sided noninferiority within .05, not equality or two-sided equivalence. A lower bound above .05 supports a material deficit; a bound crossing .05 leaves that endpoint unresolved.
Report partial outcomes explicitly: three good conditional endpoints and one weak endpoint are condition-specific transfer; good conditional scores with weak marginal scores are not a generally usable constituent readout; bare success with wording failure is wording-limited. Small gaps between weak heads are uninformative about useful transfer. Strong AUROC with weak frozen decisions supports ranking only. No endpoint average can convert one of these patterns into across-condition success. All intervals remain pointwise, without a simultaneous guarantee over topics, layers or wording.

Strong isolated judgments and strong compound behavior with weak raw probes point to a limitation of the tested readout or input regime. Explain that mismatch; successful behavior is a useful result. Strong isolated judgments but weak compound behavior, alongside successful constituent decoding, motivate matched causal preparation as a test of whether accessible information is used. Raw decodability alone does not establish its accessibility in the prompted causal regime.
If isolated judgments are weak, factual knowledge or elicitation remains a live explanation; diagnose that before attributing errors to composition. If raw probes and behavior differ but the two chat layers are weak, leave other chat readouts unresolved. Incomplete or weak two-layer chat results cannot by themselves close the causal route. The isolated-correct subset is diagnostic and model/prompt-specific; keep the all-pair results visible.
Use the old sufficiency rule only with all components: upper bounds below .02 for repair-minus-selection and control-minus-selection, control OR-boundary lower bound at least .90, and supported atomic/AND retention. Apply it descriptively on D, disclosing control-layer selection and showing procedure-layer controls. If uncertainty is too wide, report unresolved sufficiency; never widen a margin to obtain the desired outcome.
Choose one followup after the checkpoint
If cross-operator transfer fails despite strong source and target decoding, jointly trained constituent heads on existing AND/OR T_C states can test whether a shared readout can be learned. Keep this outside the current critical path and record its supervision, layer choice and evaluation before fitting. Success supports a shared learned readout; failure still cannot prove none exists. The general distinction between pairwise transfer and joint learnability already appears in related work. [R10]
For intervention, test the predicted reversal: changing one constituent should matter for AND when the other is true and for OR when the other is false. Validate a locally editable representation, factual replacements, reverse and sham controls, and constituent specificity. Compare log-odds as well as probabilities to detect generic True steering and saturation. The pilot justifies further matched validation, not a causal claim. [R9]
Choose predictive pooling when binary evidence supports distinguishable prospective predictions. Freeze gain, offset, pooling and noise assumptions before ternary results. Do not choose it only because intervention preparation is difficult. Defer mixed-scope expressions and other expansions; choose one follow-up that resolves the strongest remaining ambiguity.
